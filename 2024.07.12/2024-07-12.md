Are you still troubled by our debunking of the conventional wisdom? Then consider that, for centuries, conventional wisdom also held that heavier objects fall more rapidly than lighter ones and that the heart and not the brain is the seat of consciousness. It also rejected the idea that machines could ever fly, enable people to communicate with one another across town, or create pictures of the interior of the human body. That such “wisdom” is really shortsightedness is plain to us only because some individuals were willing to ask, Is it possible that what I and other people think we know isn’t really so? This little question is one of the most useful tools in critical thinking.

We can achieve knowledge either actively or passively. We achieve it actively by direct experience, by testing and proving an idea (as in a scientific experiment), or by reasoning. When we do it by reasoning, we analyze a problem, consider all the facts and possible interpretations, and draw the logical conclusion.

We achieve knowledge passively by being told something by someone else. Most of the learning that takes place in the classroom and the kind that happens when we watch TV news reports or read newspapers or magazines is passive. Conditioned as we are to passive learning, it’s not surprising that we depend on it in our everyday communication with friends and co-workers.



您还在为我们对传统智慧的揭穿而烦恼吗？那就想想，几个世纪以来，传统智慧还认为重物比轻物下落更快，意识是由心脏而不是大脑产生的。传统智慧还否定了机器会飞、人与人之间可以跨城交流或绘制人体内部图像的观点。我们之所以明白这种 "智慧 "其实是目光短浅，只是因为有些人愿意问：我和其他人自以为知道的事情，有可能不是真的吗？这个小问题是批判性思维中最有用的工具之一。

我们可以主动或被动地获取知识。我们可以通过直接经验、检验和证明某个观点（如科学实验）或推理来主动获取知识。当我们通过推理获得知识时，我们会分析问题，考虑所有的事实和可能的解释，并得出合乎逻辑的结论。

我们是被动地接受别人告诉我们的知识。课堂上的大部分学习，以及我们观看电视新闻报道或阅读报纸杂志时的学习，都是被动的。我们习惯于被动学习，因此在与朋友和同事的日常交流中依赖于被动学习也就不足为奇了。
